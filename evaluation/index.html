How these articles are made and checked

AI helps write these explanations, but each article is reviewed manually. Before an article is published, its claims are checked against reliable sources and the article is read by a human. Here is how that works — and what those checks cannot guarantee.

  1. Drafted (AI) — AI helps create the first version of the explanation. At this stage, the goal is to explain the idea clearly for the intended reader.
  2. Checked (AI) — Claims are compared with selected reliable sources. Several AI reviewers look for claims that are unsupported or contradicted by the evidence.
  3. Approved (Human) — A human editor reads the article for clarity, usefulness, and obvious problems that an automated claims check may miss. If something needs work, the article goes back for revision.
  4. Published — Only an article that has completed the checks is published. If a correction is made later, the article must be checked again before the updated version is published.

How the check works

The first draft is not treated as automatically correct. Checkable claims are identified and compared with a selected set of supporting sources. Multiple AI models review those claims independently. If the evidence does not support a claim clearly enough, the article is revised, more or better evidence is found, or the claim is removed or narrowed before another review.

Different explanations, same standard

Some concepts may have more than one version for different readers. A simpler explanation may leave out details that appear in a more advanced version. That is fine as long as the simpler version does not become misleading or contradict the evidence. Each version is checked on its own.

What the checks cannot guarantee

A passing review means the claims checked in the article were supported by the sources used during that review. It does not mean the article is complete, permanently up to date, or free from every possible mistake. Automated review is useful for catching problems, but clarity and usefulness still require human judgment.

What happens when something is wrong

If a claim cannot be supported, the article does not simply receive a pass. The usual fix is to find better evidence, rewrite the claim so it matches what can actually be supported, or remove it. If a published article later needs a correction, the changed article goes through the checking process again before it is republished.
